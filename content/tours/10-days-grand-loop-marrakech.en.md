## Day 1: Marrakech

Your Morocco journey begins in the Red City, with a full day in the medina alongside a licensed local guide. Start at the Koutoubia, the 12th-century minaret that still sets the skyline of Marrakech, then cross into the Bahia Palace, a 19th-century residence of carved cedar ceilings, painted doors and quiet orange-tree courtyards.

Late morning, step into the Ben Youssef madrasa, the Koranic school rebuilt in the 16th century around a marble courtyard and a pool that mirrors its stucco walls. From there your guide leads you through the dyers' and spice souks, where skeins of wool hang to dry above the lanes and cumin, saffron and ras el hanout are sold by weight. Stop for lunch wherever you like.

In the afternoon, slow down in the Majorelle Garden, the cobalt-blue villa and cactus garden restored by Yves Saint Laurent. In the evening, Jemaa el-Fna fills with food stalls, musicians and storytellers, a ten-minute walk from most riads.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Wear comfortable shoes: the medina is all on foot, with uneven paving.
• In the souks, haggling is expected; start at about half the first price and keep it friendly.
• Majorelle sells timed tickets online; booking a day ahead avoids the queue.

## Day 2: Marrakech to Ait Ben Haddou

Leave Marrakech behind and climb into the High Atlas on the Tizi n'Tichka road, the highest paved pass in Morocco at about 2,260 m. The road winds past walnut trees, terraced fields and Berber villages built from the red earth of the slopes, with photo stops where the views open over the valleys and, in winter and spring, snow on the peaks.

On the southern side of the mountains the landscape turns dry and mineral, and by early afternoon you reach Ait Ben Haddou, the fortified village (ksar) listed by UNESCO. Its earthen houses and towers climb a hillside above the river and have appeared in films such as Gladiator and in Game of Thrones. After lunch, a local guide walks you through the lanes and up to the old granary at the top, the best viewpoint over the ksar and the valley.

Stay for sunset, when the walls turn deep orange, then settle into your hotel for dinner. This is a deliberately short driving day, so you see the ksar in its best light instead of in passing.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Bring a warm layer: the Tizi n'Tichka pass can be cold even in spring.
• If you get car-sick, take something before the pass; the road has many bends.
• The climb to the granary is short but steep; sturdy shoes help.

## Day 3: Kasbahs to the Dades Valley

Today follows the Road of a Thousand Kasbahs. In Ouarzazate, visit the Taourirt Kasbah, the earthen palace of the Glaoui family, with its maze of rooms, painted ceilings and rooftop views over the town and the mountains.

Continue east to the Skoura palm grove, an oasis of date palms, olive trees and old kasbahs fed by traditional irrigation channels. After lunch, the road reaches Kelaat M'Gouna and the Valley of Roses, where hedges of Damask roses line the fields; in May the harvest fills the valley with scent, and rose water and rose oil from the local distilleries make simple, light souvenirs.

In the late afternoon you enter the Dades Valley, where the river cuts through red and ochre rock and the famous rock formations rise above the road. Your hotel sits in the valley, a quiet place for dinner and an early night before the desert.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Carry some small change for entrance fees and the Taourirt Kasbah.
• If you travel in May, ask your driver about the Rose Festival in Kelaat M'Gouna.
• Dades nights are cool outside summer; keep a jumper handy for dinner.

## Day 4: Todra Gorge to Merzouga

Start the morning in the Todra Gorge, where the canyon walls rise about 300 m on either side of a clear river. The walk along the riverbed is flat and easy, and in the narrowest part the cliffs almost close over your head; climbers are often on the rock faces.

Continue east through the oases of the Tafilalet and stop for lunch on the way. In Erfoud, the gateway to the desert, the landscape flattens into hamada (stony desert) and then, on the horizon, the dunes of Erg Chebbi appear, rising up to about 150 m and changing colour through the day.

In the late afternoon, swap the car for a camel. Your caravan walks into the dunes as the light softens, timed to reach the top of a dune for sunset. At the camp, dinner is served under the stars, often followed by Berber drums around the fire. Bring only a small bag for the night; your main luggage stays in the vehicle.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Pack a small overnight bag for the camp; the rest stays safely in the vehicle.
• Wear a scarf or hat on the camel ride and bring sunglasses against the wind and sand.
• Desert nights are cold from November to March; the camp has blankets, but a fleece is welcome.

## Day 5: A day in the Sahara

A full day in the desert, without having to rush back. Wake early to watch the sunrise from the dunes, when the sand turns from grey to gold, then return to camp for breakfast.

Mid-morning, a 4x4 tour takes you around the Erg Chebbi. Share tea with a nomad family in a tent of woven goat hair and learn how they live with their herds at the edge of the dunes. Continue to Khamlia, a village of Gnawa musicians descended from West African traders, where the music, rhythmic and hypnotic, is played on drums, iron castanets and the guembri. On the way, stop where fossils, some of them hundreds of millions of years old, are cut and polished.

The afternoon is yours: rest in the shade, try sandboarding, or simply sit in the silence of the desert. Climb a dune again for sunset before dinner.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Ask your driver for the sunrise time the day before; the dunes are worth the early start.
• Bring cash for the Gnawa musicians in Khamlia and for fossils, if you'd like to buy some.
• Drink plenty of water and wear sunscreen, even when the air feels cool.

## Day 6: Merzouga to Fes

One last sunrise over the dunes, then the longest drive of the trip, north across the country, broken up by stops. The road follows the Ziz Valley, a ribbon of date palms deep in a red canyon, with viewpoints over the oasis; dates from the valley are sold along the road.

After lunch in Midelt, at the foot of the snow-capped Ayachi, the road climbs into the Middle Atlas. In the cedar forest near Azrou, Barbary macaques often come close to the road, and the old cedars are among the largest in North Africa. Stop briefly in Ifrane, a town built in the 1930s in an Alpine style, with chalets and clean streets that feel far from the Sahara you left in the morning.

You arrive in Fes in the early evening and check in to your riad inside or near the old city.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• This is the longest drive: keep water, snacks and a charger in the car.
• Don't feed the Barbary macaques; just watch them from a short distance.
• Midelt is a good lunch stop; try the local apples in season.

## Day 7: Guided day in Fes

Fes el-Bali, founded in the 9th century, is the largest living medieval city in the world, a car-free maze of more than 9,000 lanes. A licensed guide makes the difference here, both for finding your way and for understanding what you see.

Begin at the gates of the Royal Palace and in the Mellah, the old Jewish quarter, then enter the medina through Bab Boujloud, the blue gate. Visit the Bou Inania madrasa, a 14th-century school of carved cedar, stucco and zellige tilework. The guide then leads you through the souks, organised by trade: coppersmiths, weavers, slipper makers and spice sellers.

After lunch (try pastilla, the sweet and savoury pie, or bissara soup), see the Qarawiyyin, founded in 859 and considered one of the oldest universities in the world, and the Attarine madrasa. End at the Chouara tannery, where hides are dyed in stone vats as they have been for centuries; you watch from a terrace above, with a sprig of mint for the smell.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Stay with your guide in the medina; the lanes are easy to get lost in.
• Non-Muslims can't enter the Qarawiyyin mosque; your guide shows you the best views of it.
• At the tannery, take the mint leaves you are offered; the smell is strong.

## Day 8: Fes to Chefchaouen

Drive north out of Fes through rolling hills of olive groves and wheat fields into the Rif mountains. By lunchtime you reach Chefchaouen, the blue city, founded in 1471 on a slope below two peaks.

In the afternoon, wander the medina at your own pace: lanes and staircases washed in every shade of blue, small squares, potted plants on the doorsteps and the Outa el-Hammam square below the red-walled kasbah. Chefchaouen is calm and easy to explore on foot, though there are many stairs.

Towards sunset, walk up to the Spanish Mosque on the hill opposite the town (about 30 minutes on a path). From there the whole medina spreads out below you as the lights come on, one of the most beautiful views of the trip.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Chefchaouen has many stairs; light shoes with grip are ideal.
• Go up to the Spanish Mosque about an hour before sunset and bring a torch for the way down.
• Ask before photographing people; doorways and lanes make the best pictures anyway.

## Day 9: Chefchaouen to Rabat

Leave the Rif and head south-west towards the Atlantic and Rabat, Morocco's capital, a green and relaxed city that feels very different from the medinas of the past days.

In the afternoon, see the Hassan Tower, the unfinished 12th-century minaret standing among the stumps of its columns, and the Mausoleum of Mohammed V beside it. Then walk up to the Kasbah of the Udayas, a fortress above the mouth of the Bou Regreg river, with blue-and-white lanes, a quiet Andalusian garden and views over the ocean and the old town of Salé.

The evening is free to stroll along the river or the beach before dinner.

TIPS FOR TODAY
• Rabat is relaxed and safe to explore on foot in the evening.
• Dress modestly for the Mausoleum of Mohammed V; the guards in traditional uniform make a great photo.
• The Kasbah of the Udayas has a small café with mint tea and views over the river.

## Day 10: Rabat to Marrakech

The last morning of the loop: drive south from Rabat to Marrakech, about 3.5 hours on the motorway past Casablanca. You arrive in Marrakech around lunchtime, with time for a last walk in the medina, some shopping in the souks or a hammam before your flight or your next stay.

Your driver drops you at your hotel, riad or Marrakech Menara airport (about 15 minutes from the city centre).

TIPS FOR TODAY
• If you fly the same day, plan a flight from mid-afternoon onwards to keep a comfortable margin.
• Keep some dirhams for last-minute souvenirs, then change the rest back before security.
• Tell your driver your flight time on the first day, so the last day can be planned around it.
